**1/5**

The US government has no job code for an AI engineer. Or a forward deployed engineer, or an interpretability researcher. Next revision: 2028.

When official surveys report the occupational mix isn't changing, they are counting roles defined in 2018.

**2/5**

MIT's Iceberg Index counted skills instead of titles.

Tech roles the headlines cover: 2.2% of US wage value, ~$211B.

Skills AI can already perform across admin, finance and professional work: 11.7%, ~$1.2T, spread across every state.

---

**3/5**

Anthropic's economic index, across 718 occupations: the work people hand to Claude shows roughly eight months more education than the task nominally requires. That holds in 94 of every 100.

The delegated work is harder than the job description.

---

**4/5**

In 2016 Hinton gave the radiologist five years. He bet on the judgement. The paperwork went first.

Ambient scribes cut EHR time by 13.4 min/day, and the clinician now checks a draft that can invent a diagnosis.

Law, software and science are running the same sequence.

---

**5/5**

The entry job asks for judgement now, not execution. Which leaves a question for 2035: who writes the evals?

The folks who built judgement on purpose. The work that used to build it by accident is going fast.
